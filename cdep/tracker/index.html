    What is the HTB CDEP?

    The HTB Certified Detection Engineering Principal (CDEP) is Hack The Box's master-level, hands-on detection engineering certification. It tests whether you can investigate low-level attacker tradecraft on Windows and Linux and turn it into detection logic that holds up. The path covers ETW and kernel telemetry, process injection, token abuse, persistence and credential theft.

    You earn it by completing the Detection Engineer Job-Role Path (13 modules, 250 sections, rated Hard) and then passing the exam. HTB estimates the path takes 75 days (about 600 hours at 8 hrs/day), and you have to finish 100% of it before the exam unlocks. HTB treats the SOC Analyst path as assumed knowledge, strongly recommends x86/64 assembly and C/C++, and suggests earning the CDSA first.

    The exam

    The CDEP exam runs for 10 days in a dedicated lab you can reset, and Pwnbox is enough. The breach is already contained. Your job is to build the detections that would have caught each stage, validate each one against the captured telemetry in Splunk (Sysmon, ETW, ETW-TI, Windows Security and Linux auditd), and submit the value each validated rule returns as a flag. HTB's template describes the example breach as five machines: a domain controller, three Windows workstations and a Linux build server. HTB has not published the exact pass mark.

    You also upload a commercial-grade report in English within 10 days of entering the exam, following HTB's CDEP report template. For each host it walks through every technique: how the behavior works, its ATT&CK mapping, where its detections sit on the Summiting the Pyramid models, and validated SPL rules with their false positives and proof they fire. It ends with a cross-host storyline and an ATT&CK Navigator layer. An instructor grades both the points and the report. You get 2 attempts per voucher, the free retake has to start within 14 days of your feedback, and results arrive within 20 business days.

    Top tips to prepare

    • Finish the whole path first. The exam unlocks only after 100% of the Detection Engineer path, and the path runs about 600 hours. (HTB)
    • Cover the prerequisites. HTB treats the SOC Analyst path as assumed knowledge, strongly recommends x86/64 assembly and C/C++, and suggests the CDSA first. (HTB)
    • Aim high on the pyramid. The report rates each detection High, Medium or Low coverage and favors behavior-based rules that survive a rebuild of the attacker's tooling. (HTB)
    • Validate before you ship. Only rules proven to fire on the captured data count, and the value a validated rule returns is the flag. (HTB)
    • Always submit a first report. If you don't upload a report on your first attempt, you lose the free retake. (HTB)

    How to use this tracker

    • Set your start date at the top. Every day's calendar date and your pace indicator are computed from it.
    • Pick a timeline (90/110/145/220/270 days). They all cover the same curriculum, just packed into more or fewer days, and your progress carries across when you switch.
    • Tick each task as you finish it. A day is marked complete when all its tasks are done.
    • Use the Notes field on each day as a journal for telemetry sources, queries and detection logic that worked, and techniques to revisit.
    • All progress is saved in your browser. Use Export backup regularly to keep a copy.
